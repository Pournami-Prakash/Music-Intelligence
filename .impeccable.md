## Design Context

### Users

The primary audience is recruiters and music-data peers evaluating the project as a portfolio demo. They use the Atlas to explore what a large, real playlist corpus can reveal and to assess both the product thinking and the technical execution.

### Brand Personality

Investigative, playful, and credible. The interface should feel like a music-intelligence instrument: curious and rewarding to explore, while remaining precise and honest about coverage, latency, and unavailable results.

### Aesthetic Direction

Preserve the existing dark music-lab aesthetic, compact analytical typography, instrument-like panels, and restrained green accents. New operational, loading, empty, and degraded states should belong to that visual language instead of looking like generic SaaS alerts. Avoid decorative effects that obscure data or make reliability messages feel unserious.

### Design Principles

1. Make the underlying data and its provenance feel trustworthy.
2. Distinguish absent data, limited demo coverage, slow fallbacks, and system errors in plain language.
3. Reward exploration with personality without using humor at moments of failure.
4. Preserve responsiveness and legibility on small screens and at 200% zoom.
5. Meet WCAG AA for contrast, keyboard operation, focus visibility, status announcements, and reduced motion.
